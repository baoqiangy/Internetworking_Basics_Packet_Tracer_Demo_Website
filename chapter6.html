Demo 6.1 — Configure a Secure Small Wireless LAN

Demo time35–40 minutes
Devices1 wireless router/AP, 2 laptops, 1 wired PC, 1 server
Primary focusSSID, association, WPA2, DHCP

Before class

Use a wireless router or AP supported by your Packet Tracer version. Ensure laptops have wireless NIC modules.

Live demonstration procedure

  1. Connect the wireless router/AP to the wired switch and server network.
  2. Configure the wired addressing and DHCP scope. Set the SSID to Faculty-WiFi.
  3. Configure WPA2-PSK with a classroom-only passphrase. Explain why open and WEP networks are inappropriate.
  4. On Laptop0, select the wireless network and enter the correct passphrase. Show association and DHCP address assignment.
  5. On Laptop1, intentionally enter the wrong passphrase. Show failure to associate, then correct it.
  6. Ping the default gateway and browse to the internal server.
  7. Change the SSID or security key on the AP. Show that existing clients lose connectivity until their profiles are updated.
  8. Use Physical view to move a client farther away if the device supports range visualization, and discuss signal strength, obstacles, and interference.

What students should observe

Wireless clients must complete Layer 2 association and security before DHCP and IP communication can occur.

Teaching prompts

Ask students to separate the symptoms of association failure, DHCP failure, and DNS failure.

Packet Tracer limitation

Wireless propagation, interference, roaming, channel overlap, WPA3, and enterprise 802.1X behavior are simplified in Packet Tracer.

Demo 6.2 — Guest versus Employee Wireless Access

Demo time40 minutes
Devices2 APs/wireless routers, 1 router, 1 switch, 2 laptops, 2 servers
Primary focusWireless segmentation and policy

Before class

Create Employee subnet 192.168.60.0/24, Guest subnet 192.168.70.0/24, Internal Server subnet 192.168.80.0/24, and Public Server subnet 203.0.113.0/24.

Live demonstration procedure

  1. Configure separate SSIDs and DHCP scopes for Employee and Guest clients.
  2. Configure routing between all networks and verify full connectivity before security policy is applied.
  3. Create an extended ACL that denies Guest traffic to the internal server subnet but permits Guest traffic to the public server.
  4. Apply the ACL inbound on the Guest router interface.
  5. From the employee laptop, access both internal and public servers.
  6. From the guest laptop, show that the internal server fails while the public server succeeds.
  7. Use show access-lists to display hit counters and connect policy language to packet matching.

What students should observe

Identity of the SSID alone does not enforce isolation; segmentation and Layer 3 policy do.

Teaching prompts

Ask: “Where should the ACL be placed to stop unwanted traffic as early as practical?”
Prepared as an instructor demonstration guide. Adjust interface names to the Packet Tracer device model in use.
