Demo 7.1 — Hierarchical Campus Design

Demo time40 minutes
Devices2 multilayer/distribution switches, 3 access switches, endpoints and servers
Primary focusAccess, distribution, core, redundancy

Before class

Prepare a collapsed-core topology with two distribution switches and three access switches.

Live demonstration procedure

  1. Place user devices at the access layer and servers near the distribution/core layer.
  2. Add redundant uplinks from each access switch to both distribution switches. Do not configure EtherChannel yet.
  3. Label the functions of each layer: endpoint access, policy/inter-VLAN routing, high-speed aggregation.
  4. Configure VLAN trunks and management addresses. Explain oversubscription and why access ports differ from uplinks.
  5. Identify potential single points of failure: one default gateway, one server link, one ISP edge.
  6. Demonstrate modular expansion by adding a new access switch without redesigning the whole network.
  7. Compare this hierarchy with a flat chain of switches and discuss fault domains, scalability, and troubleshooting.

What students should observe

The hierarchy separates roles and makes growth and troubleshooting more predictable.

Teaching prompts

Ask students where ACLs, default gateways, and high-capacity links logically belong.

Demo 7.2 — Spanning Tree Blocks a Loop and Recovers

Demo time35–45 minutes
Devices3 switches, 2 PCs
Primary focusSTP root bridge, blocked ports, reconvergence

Before class

Connect three 2960 switches in a triangle and place one PC on two different switches.

Live demonstration procedure

  1. Wait for links to converge. Use show spanning-tree on each switch.
  2. Identify the root bridge by the lowest bridge ID. Point out root ports, designated ports, and the alternate/blocking port.
  3. Explain what would happen without STP: broadcasts and unknown unicasts could circulate indefinitely.
  4. Generate a ping between PCs and show that normal traffic uses the forwarding tree.
  5. Change the intended distribution switch priority to 4096 and watch the root bridge election change.
  6. Disconnect a forwarding uplink. Wait for reconvergence and ping again through the formerly blocked path.
  7. Reconnect the link and discuss why STP intentionally sacrifices one link in the steady state to preserve redundancy safely.

Command reference

show spanning-tree
configure terminal
spanning-tree vlan 1 priority 4096
end
show spanning-tree vlan 1

What students should observe

One redundant link is logically blocked. After a failure, STP changes port roles and restores connectivity.

Teaching prompts

Ask students to predict the root bridge before running the command.
